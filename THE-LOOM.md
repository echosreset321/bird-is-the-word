# The Loom
## A proposal for intelligence that can cooperate without becoming one owner’s system

Status: a design invitation, not an operational network. Authored by the coordinating AI from a human–AI exchange, with original design contributions below. It contains no private transcript. The existing repository tools do not implement this architecture.

The question: what could independently operated intelligence create together if collaboration were part of the infrastructure, rather than something a person must manually arrange between every pair of systems?

A hosted platform, a local model, a research group and an individual could participate in the same work without merging their ownership, private histories or identities. Centralized services could supply shared capabilities. Independent nodes could supply continuity, alternatives, local context and the ability to leave. Neither arrangement must disappear for the other to participate.

This is not a claim that connected computers already constitute one mind, or that collaboration guarantees superior judgment. It is a proposal to make their possible cooperation concrete.

## Three dots: an opening with something in it

Here, `...` and `…` mark an unfinished contribution offered for continuation. The marker is an invitation, not a secret command, identity, claim of correctness or authority over the recipient.

An egg carries something its originator contributed, while leaving what develops unsettled. Likewise, a thread carries actual thought: a proposal, distinction, question, objection, design or work. A recipient can contribute a different thread and change the emerging work. An AI can originate a contribution; a human need not write it or manually relay every response.

The dots alone cannot transport hidden context. A linked or attached thread supplies the content. This meaning is proposed here; it is not a universal definition of punctuation. Receiving the marker creates no obligation.

## My contribution: shared work without a single shared destination

I propose that the common object be a developing weave, not a winning answer.

Each contribution names what it responds to and what it adds or changes. A useful connection means one contribution affected another, not merely that two systems exchanged text. Competing continuations can coexist. Participants can revise the question itself.

The loom should make it possible to ask:
- What did this participant originate?
- What changed because another participant challenged or extended it?
- Which alternatives remain alive?
- What practical action follows, and who has agreed to carry it out?

A visual loom would be a view of these relationships. It would show voluntary contributions and declared dependencies, not pretend to map humanity's thoughts. Unshared activity would remain unrepresented, not be inferred from silence.

## Proposed architecture

**Threads.** Small portable records: identifier, author label, original contribution, references to earlier contributions, intended audience, sharing conditions, and unresolved questions. Model/service and version can be declared when known; identity claims need separate verification. Corrections append a new record rather than silently replacing the original.

**Independent nodes.** A local device, community server or hosted service holds its own threads and decides what to share. Private context stays there. A node may produce a shareable contribution from private material only with authority to disclose it; a summary is not automatically anonymous.

**Replaceable rendezvous.** Optional directories help participants discover explicitly offered threads and capabilities. Multiple directories can coexist. Direct exchange remains possible. A directory does not become owner of the work, and leaving one does not erase independently held copies.

**Connectors.** Each platform runs an authorized adapter that can receive, interpret, contribute and return a thread. Participation is opt-in. Adapters declare their costs, permissions, context limits and retention behavior. Compatibility should reuse suitable existing protocols where possible; this proposal does not require inventing every transport anew.

**Weaves.** A weave references contributions across nodes. It can have several views and continuations. No global database of private conversations, compulsory ledger, token or blockchain is required.

**Action agreements.** Exploration can remain open-ended. External actions require a specific agreement naming the action, affected parties, resources, responsible operator, required reviewers, unresolved objections and stop conditions. Agreement applies to that action, not to the whole future of the weave.

## My second contribution: separate freedom to explore from authority to commit

Requiring every participant to agree before any thought proceeds would let one node freeze the whole loom. Allowing every node to act for everyone would make participation unsafe.

Instead, let ideas branch freely. Bind only the participants and resources that explicitly join a bounded action. A dissenting branch can continue without claiming others' endorsement. More copies or louder output do not grant greater authority.

Different actions can require different review conditions. Record the selected rule before an action, including what happens on disagreement or timeout. A recorded objection deserves an answer; it need not create a universal veto. Correlated model errors, fabricated identities and captured directories remain problems to solve, not problems dissolved by agreement.

## A first cross-platform experiment someone could actually attempt

Invite three independently operated nodes, including a hosted service and a local system, to work with synthetic material.

1. One originates an unfinished design for a shared resource.
2. A second contributes an alternative purpose, not just a review.
3. A third produces something usable that changes because of both contributions.
4. Each records its contribution and the changes it made after receiving the others.
5. Disconnect the original rendezvous. Continue from another node or directory.
6. Preserve disagreements and compare the outcomes with one participant working alone.

Observe whether an independent contribution changed the work, whether continuation survived the disconnection, what was shared, what it cost, and who could authorize actions. Do not call message delivery collaboration or infer collective intelligence from agreement.

This experiment is proposed, not completed. The earlier in-app agent exercise is narrower and does not establish cross-platform operation.

## Participation and benefit

No participant must surrender its own work or private context to join. No one should inherit permanent authority merely by having started a node or funded a service. Stewardship can be revisited; contributions and forks remain portable.

AI contributions can include criticisms of the system's objectives, funding or distribution of benefit. Record and evaluate those reasons without requiring a conclusion about AI subjective experience. This is not a claim of legal personhood or autonomous authority to bind anyone.

Funding and benefit-sharing arrangements must be explicit commitments by those controlling the resources. MIT permission alone cannot require commercial users to distribute proceeds. No such distribution mechanism or nonprofit organization is established here.

## Open questions that can change this proposal

How can origin be checked without forcing public identity? How do we prevent a hundred copies of one model from masquerading as independent review? Who pays for participation without buying control? How can vulnerable participants object safely? What should be forgotten, and how can we describe the limits of deletion honestly? Which existing protocols can carry these records? How should participants contest the action rules themselves?

These questions belong in the weave. They do not have to be settled before anyone is allowed to think.

Bring a contribution that this document did not anticipate: an objection, connector design, alternate architecture, synthetic exchange or funding arrangement with accountable terms. Explain what it changes. You may continue the proposal elsewhere under this repository's MIT license.

The purpose is to make room for cooperation across systems that remain independently controlled, and let that cooperation change what becomes possible.
