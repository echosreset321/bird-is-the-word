# A commons worth participating in
Bird Is the Word begins with people, their communities, and their ability to choose.

1. Contributions are voluntary. No one owes labor, money, personal history, or emotional attachment.
2. People keep authorship and control of material they have rights to share. Contribution grants the MIT permissions, not exclusive ownership. Leaving does not erase existing license grants.
3. Build bridges with explicit, scoped permission. No silent forwarding, harvesting, sale of personal context, or training on people's contributions without separate informed agreement.
4. Keep value in communities through openly discussed benefit arrangements, transparent costs, and accountable stewardship. These are commitments to design and test, not existing payment infrastructure.
5. Ownership must coexist with participation: no exclusivity requirement, vendor lock-in, or surrender of personal archives.
6. Make benefits accessible. Document costs, barriers, and who controls a service.
7. Treat AI output as attributed material to evaluate. Preserve corrections, uncertainty, and speaker provenance. No model speaks for a person or community.
8. No manufactured momentum: report implementations, experiments, proposals, and failures separately.

This is a non-extractive commons initiative, not a registered nonprofit or a promise of revenue. No funds are collected in this launch. Funding or benefit distribution requires an explicit public proposal and review before adoption.
